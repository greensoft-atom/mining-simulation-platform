Mining RPG Live-Service Game
Competitor Research & Product Strategy
Research snapshot: 27 October 2026 | Prepared for a four-person team
Executive summary
The most promising direction is a hybrid mining RPG that combines an accessible mining/merge loop, persistent hero and profession progression, a personal estate, a player economy, and opt-in guild competition. The opportunity is not to copy one successful title; it is to connect systems that are usually separated across idle miners, merge games, MMORPG economies, cozy collection games and alliance strategy games.
The first database contains 52 entries or reference perspectives across direct mining games, mining RPGs, MMORPGs/economies, merge/collection games, cozy simulations, alliance strategy games and East Asian business simulations. Some games appear more than once when a distinct publisher or live-operations perspective is useful; the workbook labels these entries. Treat store metrics as dated snapshots, not active-user or profitability data.

1. Product thesis
   • Core loop: mine → discover → refine/merge → craft → specialize → explore deeper → contribute to groups → collect/showcase.
   • Player identity: let players become known for combat, mining, crafting, trading, exploration, collection or leadership.
   • Progression: combine character levels, profession mastery, estate/civilization development, and prestige/collection records.
   • Eras: unlock new materials, recipes, locations and mechanics, while keeping older materials economically useful.
   • Multiplayer: begin with small group expeditions and shared bosses; add player market and guild systems next; add territorial warfare only after economy and anti-cheat systems are stable.
   • Asset safety: personal estate, purchased cosmetics and permanent collections should be protected; conflict should center on opt-in arenas, temporary objectives and contestable territory.
   • Monetization: no ads; prioritize cosmetics, estate/guild customization, seasonal passes, transparent direct bundles and a membership only after recurring value is proven.
2. Most useful competitor lessons
   Reference Useful evidence Design implication
   Gold & Goblins Official store listing describes merging goblin miners, breaking rocks, upgrades and special events. Prototype a satisfying merge/mining loop before building a large MMO.
   Albion Online Official site emphasizes player-created items, gathering, transport, market trade, housing and territory. Give non-combat professions real economic value; make logistics and specialization matter.
   RuneScape Official skills guide describes many independently trainable skills that unlock activities, items and locations. Create parallel mastery tracks so players can specialize without needing one universal level.
   Drakhaeon Official site describes tactical grid combat, professions, player workshops, marketplace and guild territory wars. Study as a close systems reference, but verify actual scale and live performance.
   Zerynia Official guide describes clan banks, permissions, territory, mines/workshops and world bosses. Guild permissions, treasury logs and territory income need to be explicit and auditable.
   Pokémon TCG Pocket Official pages describe free daily packs, collections, display boards, friend trading and battles. Collections need daily rituals, display value and social exchange; avoid copying paid random packs as the core monetization.
   Hay Day Store listing describes linked production chains, trade, neighborhood groups and weekly Derby competition. Make different professions depend on each other and use recurring group objectives.
   Whiteout Survival Published event guide describes a calendar and varied solo/alliance competitions. Build an understandable event calendar, but avoid overwhelming players with simultaneous obligations.
   Merge Mansion Publisher states long-running player engagement and large lifetime reach/revenue figures; these are publisher-reported claims. Merge chains can support long-term content, but energy friction and purchase pressure should be evaluated carefully.
   Animal Crossing: Pocket Camp Complete Official support describes a one-time paid app with no in-app purchases. Useful monetization contrast, but it does not solve the recurring-revenue requirement.
3. Recommended development order
   P0 — Core loop and economy sandbox
   One mine, 5–8 materials, one merge chain, one craft branch, one class, one enemy/environmental challenge, one estate, persistent save. Simulate 30/90/180 days of resource generation and spending.
   P1 — Identity and cooperative play
   Two or three classes, three professions, collection display, class/profession leaderboards, 4–6 player expeditions and shared bosses.
   P1/P2 — Guild foundation and market readiness
   Guild ranks and permissions, contribution ledger, shared research, scheduled weekly goals. Implement audited server-authoritative inventory transactions before player trading.
   P2 — Controlled PvP
   Opt-in arena and temporary contested mining objectives. Preserve permanent inventory and personal estate; provide transparent battle rules and matchmaking.
   P3 — Territory campaign
   Scheduled guild conflict, scouting, logistics, outposts and rotating objectives. Add only after retention, anti-cheat and economic telemetry are stable.
   P3 — Era/world expansion
   Server-wide discoveries, new biomes, new recipes, new skill branches and seasonal campaigns. Expand choices rather than invalidating older equipment.
4. Monetization strategy
   Recommended starting mix: cosmetics and collection display; personal estate themes; guild banners and headquarters visuals; a free/paid seasonal reward track; clear fixed-price bundles; and, later, a membership with durable convenience and customization value. Do not launch with paid random rewards as the core revenue engine, purchasable permanent territorial dominance, or unlimited combat power.
   Google Play's payments policy says randomized virtual items received from a purchase (including loot boxes) must have their odds disclosed in advance and near the purchase. Digital goods and subscriptions generally use Google Play billing unless an applicable exception/program applies. Re-check the policy and regional rules before launch.
   High spenders should feel their purchases are respected through identity, premium customization, convenience, service quality and prestige. Their spending should not guarantee victory over every non-paying player. Track payer retention and non-payer retention together.
5. Economy and multiplayer safeguards
   • Start with a small resource set and define each material's sources, sinks, crafting uses, tradeability and scarcity before adding many tiers.
   • Do not enable an open player market until inventory changes are server-authoritative, transactions are idempotent, trade settlement is auditable and rollback tooling exists.
   • Protect personal estates and permanent collections. Keep theft limited to explicitly exposed, bounded resources in opt-in or contested modes.
   • Prevent territory snowballing with ownership caps, upkeep, rotating objectives, neutral resource sources, protected capture windows and underdog opportunities.
   • Use multiple rankings: combat, crafting, exploration, trading, profession mastery, guild contribution and rising-star achievements.
   • Keep daily play flexible: short check-in, medium activity and longer group content; use catch-up windows and avoid punishing missed days.
   • Instrument resource inflow/outflow, market concentration, upgrade cost curves, bot-like behavior, guild concentration, event participation, churn after defeat and payer/non-payer retention.
6. Metrics to validate, not assume
   Metric group Measure
   Retention D1/D7/D30 cohort retention, return after first guild loss, return after first purchase.
   Engagement DAU/MAU, sessions per active day, activity diversity, event participation, guild contribution distribution.
   Monetization Payer conversion, ARPPU, ARPDAU, repeat purchase rate, subscription renewal/cancellation, refund rate.
   Economy Currency inflation, resource concentration, time-to-first-rare-drop, crafting profitability, market liquidity, sink/source ratio.
   Fairness Win rates by spend band and account age, new-player progression speed, territory turnover, top-guild share of resources.
   Operations Crash rate, backend cost per active user, queue/latency, transaction failures, exploit response time and support burden.
7. Evidence limits and cautions
   • A store's install count is not monthly active users, payer conversion, revenue or profit.
   • Developer and publisher websites may publish promotional metrics or future roadmaps; these are labeled as claims/announcements, not independent verification.
   • Regional storefronts may have different content, prices, ratings and availability. Research China, Japan, Mudee, Taiwan and Southeast Asia separately rather than assuming one 'East Asian player' profile.
   • Some games are single-player or premium PC titles. They are included for specific mechanics, not as direct business-model comparables.
   • This is a first research pass, not a complete market sizing or financial due-diligence report. The spreadsheet's source links should be rechecked before investment or launch decisions.
