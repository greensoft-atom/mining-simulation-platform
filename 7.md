# What the research suggests we should do next

My strongest recommendation is to focus on five interconnected systems.

### 1. Mining and merging

Start with a satisfying mining loop, branching resource upgrades, crafting and meaningful discoveries. Gold & Goblins is a particularly useful reference for merging and mining, while Hollow Mine offers ideas for connecting destructible terrain, combat and crafting. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.redcell.goldandgoblins&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2. Distinctive heroes and professions

Create several ways to become valuable: combat, mining, crafting, trading, exploration and leadership. Study games such as RuneScape for independent skill progression and Drakhaeon for the combination of professions, tactical combat and player workshops. [runescape.com](https://www.runescape.com/game-guide/skills?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 3. A functioning player economy

Before introducing unrestricted player trading, define where resources come from, what consumes them, how crafting costs work and how transactions are audited. Albion Online is a useful reference for how gathering, manufacturing, transportation and trade can depend on one another. [Albion Online](https://albiononline.com/home?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 4. Guilds and territorial competition

Begin with group expeditions and shared objectives. Introduce contested mining areas before permanent territorial warfare. Keep personal estates safe and provide smaller guilds with ways to compete.

### 5. A sustainable no-ad business model

Prioritize cosmetics, estate customization, guild identity, seasonal passes and transparent direct purchases. Introduce membership only when it provides continuing value.

For Android distribution, monetization design also needs to account for Google Play's rules on digital purchases, subscriptions and disclosure of randomized-item odds. [Google Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## One important strategic conclusion

I would **not** try to build the complete game described in our vision as the first release.

The first version should prove that players enjoy mining, merging, crafting, character development and collecting. The next version should prove that they return and enjoy cooperating. Only then should we invest heavily in player trading and territorial warfare.

This sequence reduces the risk of spending months building a sophisticated multiplayer world around a core loop that has not yet demonstrated that it is fun.

The database is a starting research asset, not the final word on the market. The next useful research stage is a **deep-dive comparison of approximately 10–12 priority games**, examining their progression systems, event structures, purchase models, player complaints, social mechanics and long-term content strategies in greater detail. That will help us turn the broad vision into a concrete game design and a realistic development plan.
